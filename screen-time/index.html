How it works

A real economy, running on their iPad.

You set the prices once. After that the earning, the spending, and the locking all happen without you.

Make the bed, finish the homework, read the chapter. Each approved mission pays the XP you assigned to it.

2

3

Why it changes behavior

Chosen, not endless.

When screen time is free, more is always the answer. When it costs something they earned, your child starts doing the math themselves.

  • ✓The nagging ends. You're not the timer anymore. The timer is the timer.

FAQ

Questions parents ask

What happens when the timer runs out?
The apps lock again, automatically. Your child can buy more minutes if they have the XP, or go earn more. Nobody has to come take the iPad.
Can I still give free screen time?
Yes, two ways. You can set a free daily baseline of minutes per child that needs no XP at all, and you can grant bonus time from your own phone any time — it reaches their device wherever you are.
Do different apps really cost different amounts?
Yes. There are three tiers: free apps that never lock (your pick — reading apps, Duolingo, music practice), everyday apps at your standard rate, and Expensive Apps at a steeper rate with no free minutes. You set every price, per child.
What actually enforces the timer?
Apple's own Family Controls, at the system level. The lock survives app-closing and restarts, and you never touch the Settings app. You can read more about how the lock works.
Can my child overspend or go negative?
No. They can only spend XP they've already earned. When the balance runs out, the way to more screen time is more missions — that's the point.
Is this a Premium feature?
Yes. Earning screen time with XP is part of Premium, which is $8.99/month or $89.99/year after a 7-day free trial. The free plan keeps the simple version: one child, and the apps unlock when the day's chores are done. See pricing.
